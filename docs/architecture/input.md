# Input and controls

Every feature ships with both input paths, touch and keyboard, mouse or gamepad (AGENTS.md).

## From the page to the engine

- `static/input.js` reads pointer events (mouse, pen, touch), the keyboard by `KeyboardEvent.code` and Standard-mapping gamepads polled each frame. It forwards them as `{type: "input", event}` (format in `platforms/web/src/input.rs`) only while the engine owns the screen; otherwise the DOM interface keeps them. The engine turns this on with the `input_capture` op and tells the page with `{type: "capture", on}`.
- `volya-engine-core::input` reduces every source to the menu's mouse and keyboard vocabulary: touch is a touchpad wherever the game's cursor shows (below), elsewhere the touch point and the left button; a gamepad is a mouse wherever the game's cursor shows ([below](#gamepad-as-a-mouse)), elsewhere the arrows (d-pad, left stick), Enter (south) and Escape (east). `input_state` reports it.
- Keys pressed with Control reach the engine only while the character takes keys (the level's controls live, or the inventory screen or the PDA window over them): Control is the crouch key. Chrome keeps Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T and Ctrl+N from a page outside full screen with Keyboard Lock (documented, not measured), hence [Full screen](#full-screen-while-playing).

## Touchpad

A Volya Engine addition, decided by the user (#108): in the standard mobile mode touch is a touchpad for the cursor wherever the game shows it (`App::cursor_shown`: the title menu and its screens, options, the menu during a game, save and load screens, message boxes, the on-screen keyboard, the inventory screen, the PDA window). A touch never clicks where it lands there, text fields included. The ergonomic mode clicks where it lands instead ([below](#direct-clicks-and-the-screens-zoom)).

- `static/touchpad.js` is a pure recognizer (our values):
  - a swipe anywhere moves the cursor by its motion;
  - a tap (lifted within 500 ms, moved at most 10 CSS pixels) clicks at the cursor;
  - a touch beginning less than 250 ms after a tap's lift (the engine's double-click time, `DOUBLE_CLICK_MS`) is a second touch: lifted as a tap it is a double click; moved past the slop or held 500 ms it is a single press held until lifted, so double-tap and hold, then move, drags (sliders, scroll bars, list thumbs) and a drag never acts as a double click (it never loads a save);
  - the button a drag holds is tracked apart from the gesture and released when its finger lifts, on a cancelled touch and on a reset (blur, the cursor hiding, capture changing);
  - two fingers swiped up or down scroll, one wheel step per 40 CSS pixels of their mean travel, content following the fingers; pinched, one wheel step per 40 CSS pixels of the change of their distance, apart as wheel down (the PDA map's zoom in, #743), whichever they first do past the tap slop (a pinch changing the distance more than twice the mean travel) going on while both stay down;
  - a long press (500 ms, Android's timeout, within 10 pixels) is a right click, at the timer or at the lift if that comes first.
- It sends semantic messages: `cursor_motion` with the swipe's speed smoothed over 75 ms, `cursor_button` with the recognizer's double-click flag, `wheel`. The engine keeps the touchpad's buttons apart from direct touch's (`Source::Touchpad`), applies the acceleration (`volya-engine-core::input::touchpad_gain`: one to one below 200 CSS px/s, rising linearly to 2.5 times at 1200, level beyond), then the menu's motion rule (title-menu.md section 7).
- The mode follows `cursor_shown`, told to the page with `{type: "cursor", shown}`. A touchpad touch stays one until lifted; direct touches still down when the cursor appears are dropped, their button released without moving the cursor (`touch_drop`).
- While the cursor shows the page cancels `touchend`, which suppresses the compatibility mouse events and the click (documented: Touch Events specification), so none blurs a text entry just focused.

## Gamepad as a mouse

The user's design (#835): wherever the game shows its cursor (`App::cursor_shown`, the touchpad's screens) the gamepad drives the game's own cursor as a mouse does, the virtual cursor of console ports of mouse screens (Xbox's mouse mode for apps: documented, its button map a diagram; Roblox's gamepad cursor: documented). No screen moves the cursor between items by itself. The app turns it on at the end of each frame (`Input::set_pad_cursor`, off while the save screen's on-screen keyboard takes the gamepad) and runs its frame before the screens read theirs (`Input::pad_cursor_frame`), with the showing screen's clickable rectangles (each screen's `cursor_targets`); the cursor's motion is a mouse's (`Action::Cursor`), so drags, hover and the double click work unchanged, and it draws the cursor the ergonomic mode hid until the next touch. In the level nothing changes ([below](#the-levels-controls)). Its values (`volya-engine-core::input`):

- The left stick moves the cursor in its direction at a speed by its deflection: a scaled radial dead zone of 0.24 (XInput's `XINPUT_GAMEPAD_LEFT_THUMB_DEADZONE`, 7849 of 32767, and its guide's rescaling past it: documented), then 1,000 UI units a second (about a screen's width) times the square of the fraction of the way out (our choice; XInput's guide suggests a non-linear curve, a cube for driving, for precision at low deflection): 40 units/s a fifth of the way out, 250 halfway. A frame counts 100 ms at most.
- Friction: over a clickable element (a button, a list row, a cell item, a slider's thumb, a tab, a map tool) the cursor moves at 0.4 of that speed (our value; Roblox's and Xbox's browser cursors slow over interactable elements, documented without numbers). No pull toward an element: the cursor goes only where the stick sends it.
- South (A / cross) is the left button, held as long as it is (drags), a second press within 250 ms a double click; west (X / square) the right button (the inventory's context menu); east (B / circle) stays Escape, and each screen's back (the key bound to `quit` where the screen has it). North stays the inventory key.
- The d-pad nudges the cursor 4 UI units a press, repeating every 50 ms after 400 ms held (our values): precise placement without the stick. It has no other meaning on these screens.
- The shoulders are the wheel, left up and right down, repeating every 100 ms after 400 ms held; the right stick too, pushed up wheel up, past a dead zone of 0.265 (XInput's right-stick value), a step at once, then up to 12 a second at full tilt (our value). The PDA's map keeps the right stick for moving the map (our design, #743), so there it scrolls nothing.
- Buttons held as the mode changes are released as they were pressed.

## Direct clicks and the screen's zoom

The user's design for the ergonomic mobile mode (#807; the Settings choice of [the level's controls](#the-levels-controls), stored as `experimental`): on the screens that show the game's cursor touch is no touchpad.

- `static/direct-touch.js` is a pure recognizer in page coordinates (our values):
  - a touch is a direct mouse click where it lands: down presses the left button there, a moving finger drags it, the lift releases it, all as the level's direct touches (`["touch", …]`, `Event::Touch`), so a second tap within 250 ms is a double click by the engine's own rule;
  - the press waits until the finger moves past the tap slop (10 CSS pixels), lifts, or has been down 100 ms, and is then made where the finger went down, with its down time: a second finger landing meanwhile makes a pinch, never a click (as a scroll view delays its content's touches; UIScrollView's 150 ms is believed, not documented). A finger joining a press already made, or a third finger, does nothing;
  - two fingers pinch the whole screen's picture: magnified by their distance over the one they began at, 1 to 4 times, the picture's point first under their midpoint kept under it (so the midpoint moving drags the picture), always covering the screen: an edge stops the picture until the fingers come back (reckoned from the pinch's start, so the edge doesn't shift it). Neither finger clicks; one lifted leaves the other doing nothing until it lifts. A pinch is not the wheel here (the PDA map zooms by its own buttons, #803).
- The zoom is a CSS transform (`translate`, `scale`, origin at the top left) on `#screen`, the element holding the canvas, so the picture is magnified as drawn (no sharper). Positions reach the engine in the canvas's own CSS pixels, read back through the canvas's on-screen box (`getBoundingClientRect` includes ancestors' transforms: documented, CSSOM View), so the engine knows nothing of the zoom. It ends when the cursor hides (a level's controls going live, the inventory or PDA closing), when capture changes, on leaving the mode and on a resize (our choice).
- The game draws no cursor: the page tells the engine (`["touch_direct", on]`, `Event::TouchDirect`) on a touch device (a coarse pointer, touch points, or a touch seen), and the frame drops the `cursor` sprites (`Input::cursor_drawn`) until a mouse or a gamepad moves the cursor; a touch hides it again. Hover hints without a cursor: #804.
- The save screen's name field: a direct click's lift over the field (by the field's reported place) focuses the page's text entry in that pointerup handler, a user activation (documented: HTML's activation-triggering events).

## The level's controls

- `volya-engine::controls` maps every path onto the game's movement actions: keyboard and mouse by the game's bindings, gamepad sticks and buttons, and the on-screen touch controls. The engine owns their logic (the sprint lock, `SprintLock`; the look curve over the drag speed the page measures, `look_gain`; tap-to-move, `TapMove`; direct manipulation look, `TouchLookDrag`).
- Touch has two sets of controls, a radio choice in the app's Settings (on touch devices; the user's decision, #802), stored with the app's other settings (`__settings.json` in the settings cache, `touch`: `standard` or `experimental`, update.js's `saveSettings` writing only its own keys). The page applies it when the level's controls go live, and at once while they are (Settings shows only while no game runs, so in practice at the next level). Each sends its own messages (`stick`, `stick_cancel`, `stick_lock`, `look_delta`; `look`), and the engine acts on whichever arrive, so it needs no mode of its own.
- Standard (the default): the move stick on the left half and look drags on the right, as before the experiments. The stick appears where a thumb lands, sends its reach in stick radii, sprints pushed forward past its rim and locks the sprint let go on the target above it (`SprintLock`, controls.rs).
- The look curve (#747, the user's choice): 3.25 mouse counts per CSS pixel up to 300 px/s, so a slow drag across the look half turns about 180°, rising linearly to 6.5 at 2,000 px/s, level beyond: the original's 1,000-DPI mouse at the default sensitivity, about 137.5° per inch of thumb travel (about 153 CSS px an inch, believed), which a quick flick across the look half nears; no inertia (the view stops with the finger). Thumb jitter of 2–4 px (believed) turns 1–2° at the base gain. The counts turn by `mouse_sens` as a mouse's do (0.1375° per count at the default 0.12). Measured by game-input's look-swipe test at the default sensitivity, a swipe of the controls' width at 250 px/s, 1,000 px/s and in 0.2 s, and a drag across the look half at 250 px/s and in 0.15 s: iPhone 17 Pro Max Simulator, upright (turned; 796 px, half 398): 356°, 484°, 672°, 178°, 305°; desktop Chrome (756 px, half 378): 338°, 459°, 636°, 169°, 285°. Before (#731: 1 up to 300 px/s, 3.25 at 3,000), the iPhone's width turned 109°, 166°, 306° and its half's flick 113°. Landscape is not measured (the Simulator runs upright); its controls are believed 956 px wide, so a slow drag across its look half would turn 214°.
- Ergonomic (stored as `experimental`): tap-to-move (#772) with direct manipulation look (#754) and a pinch that zooms the view (#807, below). The whole picture is one area where a tap moves and a drag looks. The page sends each finger's down, moves, lift or cancel with its place in the picture (from the centre, in half its height), its event time and half the picture's height in CSS pixels; a finger dragged from Jump or Crouch comes as already dragging (`drag`). The engine tells them apart (`TouchLookDrag`): a finger that stays within 10 CSS pixels of where it went down (the touchpad's tap slop) doesn't look, and lifted there within 500 ms it is a tap; once past the slop it is a drag for good, grabbing the scene from where it went down (a one-time step of up to the slop, about 1.7° at a 440-px-high picture, so the scene still stays under the finger).
- Tap-to-move (`TapMove`, our design):
  - A tap's ray from the eye through its place in the picture (the view of the frame before it, the one shown) meets the level's collision geometry, either side, the nearest solid triangle within 100 m (pass-through ones, invisible walls among them, let it by; our choice); the hit is the destination. A tap that meets nothing ends the trip.
  - The trip follows a route to the reachable floor nearest the destination ([simulation.md](simulation.md#navigation), #805): around walls, crouched or in the low crouch under low ceilings, jumping up ledges and across gaps or low obstacles, up and down ladders. Its search runs on the tap's frame, 600 expansions a frame, so a long one spreads over frames while the character waits. Without a route (no floor under the feet, or none nearer the destination than the start) it runs straight there.
  - The character runs each leg as if forward were held toward the next waypoint: the movement layer's own physics, collisions, footsteps and climbing, the direction keys turned by the bearing instead of the camera's yaw (`Request::toward`, player-movement.md 5.3's heading). A route's crouch holds `crouch` (running for the crouch, walking for the low crouch, 4.2), taken 0.7 m before its leg and let go after it, the movement standing up once there's room; a jump is pressed once running at 2 m/s (or after 0.5 s on its leg) within 1.1 m of a ledge's floor or 0.45 m of a gap's edge, again after 0.7 s if it failed; on a ladder's legs the trip turns the camera to face it, looking up 0.3 rad to climb up and down 0.7 rad to climb down (climbing.md 2.1, 2.5), at most 6 rad/s, unless a finger is on the picture. Otherwise a trip runs, or creeps while the low crouch holds touch's walk latch (`follow`, our values).
  - It ends at its route's end or with the feet within 0.3 m of the destination (3D); stuck (its waypoint no 5 cm nearer for 700 ms) or pushed 1.5 m off its leg it plans again, up to 3 times, and stuck after that it ends; a straight trip ends when its distance hasn't shrunk by 5 cm for 500 ms (stuck against a wall). It also ends on a new tap (a new destination), when a key or the gamepad moves, when a screen covers the level or the controls hide, on a blur and on a frame over 250 ms from the previous one (our values).
  - A double tap (the second down within 250 ms of the first's lift, the engine's double-click time, and within 40 CSS pixels of it) sprints that trip by `sprint_toggle`; the movement layer's rules still decide, and a sprint they cancel (a crouch, 4.3) isn't asked for again that trip. A third tap starts afresh.
  - A tap on the destination's marker, within 23 CSS pixels of it (the ring's 13-pixel radius plus the tap slop; our choice), cancels the trip and the character stops: for an accidental tap, or when danger shows up (the user's request, #772). A double tap's second tap there still sprints; only a tap after the double tap's 250 ms cancels.
  - Measured by game-input on the start level's floor: a tap 1.46 m ahead (desktop Chrome) ended its trip 0.27 m from the destination and came to rest 0.24 m from it; 1.02 m ahead (iPhone 17 Pro Max Simulator) 0.27 m and 0.27 m. A trip stops where the keyboard's walk would: on the start level something 2.3 m behind the start stops both.
  - Looking doesn't end a trip (our choice): it steers by world position, not by the view, so the player can look around on the way.
  - While a trip is under way the touch controls mark its destination with a plain ring (our own interface, not the game's), placed where the engine projects it (`trip_marker`, `{type: "trip", at}`) and hidden when it is off the picture or behind the eye.
- Experimental touch look is direct manipulation with momentum, after iOS's scroll views and Maps (#754).
  - While a finger drags, the scene's direction under it stays under it (WWDC 2018 "Designing Fluid Interfaces": one-to-one tracking, documented): pitch solved from the direction's elevation, then yaw from its azimuth, at the game's 67.5° vertical field of view over the picture's height (`grab_turn`), so near the middle a CSS pixel turns tan(33.75°) / half the height (about 0.17° on a 440-px-high phone, believed). Dragging right turns the view left, dragging down looks up: the scene moves with the finger, the opposite of a mouse's and the mobile shooters' look. `mouse_sens` and `mouse_invert` don't apply: either would take the scene off the finger. At the pitch limits the pitch stops and the yaw still follows.
  - The lift's velocity is the least-squares slope of the finger's turn over its last 100 ms of moves, a pause of 40 ms between moves starting it afresh and a lift 80 ms after the last move flinging nothing (Chromium's and Android's velocity trackers, documented in their sources; iOS's window is unpublished), at most 720°/s (our choice, as Android caps a fling).
  - The momentum keeps 0.998 of its speed a millisecond (UIScrollView's `.normal` rate; believed: read from UIKit at run time, Apple documents no value), integrated over each frame's real time (v (1 − r^dt) / −ln r), so it turns v / −ln r in all at any frame rate (the WWDC talk's projection v r / (1 − r), within 0.1%), and ends once what is left would turn under 0.05°. Its pitch part ends at a pitch limit. A finger put down to look stops it (documented for UIScrollView), as does a cancelled touch, the controls hiding, a screen over the level, a blur, or a frame over 250 ms from the controls' previous one (our choices). Maps' own gain beyond momentum is unpublished; none is added. Measured by game-input's look-drag test from the right half's centre, slow drags held still within 1% (or 0.2°) of the scene staying under the finger and flicks within 15% of the drag plus v / −ln r: iPhone 17 Pro Max Simulator, upright (turned; 796 × 440 px): a 0.4-half drag right −16.3° yaw, 0.3 of the height down −21.9° pitch; a flick of 318 px at 777 px/s 113° (drag 52°, coast 60°), at 2,115 px/s 234° (drag 52°, coast 166°); desktop Chrome (756 × 469): −15.8°, −21.9°; 302 px at 747 px/s 104° (47°, 56°), at 1,958 px/s 206° (47°, 147°).
- The ergonomic mode's pinch (#807, `TouchLookDrag`, `pinch_turn`): two fingers down on the picture change the field of view continuously, the tangent of half the vertical field of view scaled by their old distance over their new one (apart zooms in), within 20° and the game's 67.5° (our bounds: at most about 3.4 times the picture's magnification, never wider than the original), and turn the view so the scene's direction under their midpoint stays under it. Zooming alone scales the picture about its centre, so a pinch about the centre keeps the scene under both fingers exactly, elsewhere nearly. Neither finger taps; a third does nothing; one lifted leaves the other dragging from where it is. The field of view goes through the player's (view-projection.md 3.1, as aiming's would: `Request::fov`), so the projection, the size thresholds, the detail and particle cut-offs, tap-to-move's rays, the trip marker and the drag's grab all use it, and the mouse turns by F_view / 67.5. It resets at every level start (a new player and controls), a loaded save included. Measured by game-input: two fingers drawn from 100 to 200 CSS pixels apart about the picture's centre zoomed to 36.95° (the formula's 36.95°) with no turn, on desktop Chrome and the iPhone 17 Pro Max Simulator.
- Mouse look runs under Pointer Lock, taken on a click in the level and released by the page when the level's controls stop. A lost lock opens the menu, since the browser takes Escape to leave it.
- The lock asks for unadjusted movement (`requestPointerLock({unadjustedMovement: true})`), so turning is linear in the mouse's counts like the original's, which reads them without the system's acceleration. Documented: Chrome 88+ on macOS and Safari 18.4+ on macOS deliver unaccelerated deltas; iOS Safari has no such option. Where the browser rejects the option (`NotSupportedError`) the page takes a plain lock, with the system's accelerated movement.
- Touch controls (`static/touch-controls.js`): DOM elements over the canvas, shown on touch devices (a coarse pointer, or a touch seen) while the level's controls are live. They send semantic messages, not raw contacts: the stick's reach (past its rim too) and look drags (standard) or the fingers on the picture (experimental), buttons and the Crouch swipe. Menu at the top left, Console right of it (#642, below), Inventory at the top right (our design, #110; its glyph is our backpack, as the UI textures have no inventory icon); PDA left of it (#730; its glyph the HUD's PDA icon), which acts as the key bound to `active_jobs` and, the PDA having no close button, alone shows over the open PDA to close it (touches elsewhere reach the touchpad); Torch and Night vision in a row left of PDA (#363), each shown only while its command would do something. The page draws them, places the sprint lock's target and draws the trip's destination marker.
- They look like the game's HUD instruments (#105), drawn from its own UI textures, which the engine decodes for the page once per session (`ui_images` op through `volya-engine::ui_image`: by texture path, texture description id or an item's inventory icon, optionally cut to a rectangle or turned into a silhouette or an engraving, returned as RGBA; `static/touch-art.js`). Nothing of the game is shipped: until then, or without game data (the UX audit page), vector approximations in the same palette stand in.
- `{type: "controls"}` tells the page when the level's controls go live or stop and the toggles they show.

## Text entry without a keyboard

A Volya Engine addition, decided by the user (#94), for the save screen's name field. The field keeps its own rules (the characters its keys type, its width limit); physical keyboards type as before.

- Touch (`static/text-entry.js`): the field is clicked with the touchpad's cursor like everything else. The engine reports the field's place, focus and whether its cursor is over it (`{type: "text_field"}`; nothing while the on-screen keyboard or a box covers the screen). On touch devices the page keeps an unseen text input there that never takes taps itself (`pointer-events: none`).
- A device's keyboard rises only for an input focused within a user gesture (iOS; believed, not measured), so when a touchpad gesture presses the left button over the field, the page focuses the input in that very event handler, and the same press focuses the engine's field as a click does; if the engine doesn't focus its field within 500 ms the input is blurred again.
- Typed characters go as text (`["text", s]`, `Event::Text`), the input emptied at once; typographic quotes become plain ones; an input method's composed text is sent when composing ends; Backspace, Delete, Enter, Left and Right go as keys. The field losing focus (Enter) closes the device's keyboard.
- Gamepad: an on-screen keyboard drawn by the engine (`volya-engine::on_screen_keyboard`), flat keys over the screen's bottom: the field's character keys as on a US keyboard, Shift (a toggle), Space, Backspace and Done, the last four drawn as line symbols. South with the cursor in the field focuses it and opens the keyboard, which then takes the gamepad from the cursor (d-pad moves the highlight, south presses, west is Backspace, east is Done) until the field loses focus. While it shows, mouse and touch don't reach the screen under it; keys do.

## The console on touch screens

A Volya Engine addition, decided by the user (#642, the proposal on #609): the original opens the console only with a key and types into it only by key codes (console-window.md section 1, console-input.md section 2). Keyboards type as before; on touch devices (`static/console-touch.js`):

- A Console button (`>_`, a 44 px disc like the touch controls'): in a level right of Menu (`static/touch-controls.js`), over the main menu at the same top-left place while a key bound to `console` would open the console there (`{type: "console", open, menu}`, `App::console_opens_over_menu`). It presses `TouchButton::Console`, which opens the console where and when such a key does, and is hidden while the console is open.
- The same tap focuses an unseen text input, so the device's keyboard rises: the button acts on the tap's release, as a touch's pointerup is a user activation and its pointerdown isn't (documented: HTML's activation-triggering events; that iOS raises the keyboard there is believed, as for the save field). If the console doesn't open within 500 ms the input is blurred again.
- Typed characters go as `["typed", s]`; the engine turns each into the presses of the key that types it on the console's table (`console::typing_key`), within a left Shift press where it needs one: upper-case letters are their key, typographic quotes the plain ones, other characters (Cyrillic, `€`, emoji) do nothing. The keyboard's Backspace and Return are Backspace and Enter; its own paste is what Insert does with the pasted text.
- A key strip just above the device's keyboard (at the visual viewport's bottom; that iOS shrinks it for the keyboard is believed), upright even when the game is turned: Esc, Tab, ↑, ↓, PgUp, PgDn and Paste. Each is that key, held from press to release, so the console's own key repeat applies; Paste is Insert on its tap's release, reading the clipboard there (input.js). Strip taps take no focus from the input.
- Hiding the keyboard leaves the console open and the strip goes; a tap on the console's area (the top half) raises the keyboard again. The console closing closes it.

## Full screen while playing

A Volya Engine addition, decided by the user (#85): the game crouches on Control, and outside a full screen the page requested, with Keyboard Lock, the browser keeps Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T and Ctrl+N from the page (documented: the Keyboard Lock specification, Chrome's article). Offered, never imposed (`static/fullscreen.js`).

- The offer ("Full screen", a note that it keeps such shortcuts in the game and that holding Esc leaves it, a close control) shows in the level's top right corner, with the system pointer, where `navigator.keyboard.lock`, `requestFullscreen` and `fullscreenEnabled` exist and the pointer isn't coarse (desktop Chrome; Safari and Firefox lack the API, documented by MDN), while the level's controls are live and the mouse is free. Not on macOS (`navigator.userAgentData.platform`, else `navigator.platform`): its browser shortcuts use Command, which is never locked (our choice).
- At most once per game (our choice): once hidden (by the click that takes pointer lock, the menu, accepting or closing it) it doesn't come back until the next game. Presses on it stay with the page.
- Accepting, in the click (full screen needs a user gesture): `navigator.keyboard.lock()` with KeyW, KeyT, KeyN and Escape and every key bound to an action (the `bound_keys` op, read as the controls go live), minus Tab, F4 and the OS keys, which stay the system's even when bound (our choice); then `requestFullscreen()` on the page's root, lock first as the specification recommends, so the browser shows one exit notice. Keyboard Lock works only in a full screen the page requested, not F11's (documented).
- Under the lock a short Escape reaches the game, which opens the menu; holding it two seconds leaves full screen, and the hold begins as a press the page receives, so the menu opens on the way out (documented; not measured). Pointer lock works inside full screen (documented); whether a short Escape releases it is not measured, and either way the menu opens.
- Bindings changed while in full screen are locked anew as the controls go live again. Leaving full screen ends the lock; when the player leaves the game the page leaves the full screen it entered, then unlocks.

## The options screen's full screen

A deviation decided by the user: the options screen's "Полный экран" (`rs_fullscreen`, display-mode.md A.2, A.9) is the browser's full screen for the page, not a display mode.

- `rs_fullscreen` follows the page: the page tells the engine at start and at every `fullscreenchange` whether it is in full screen (`full_screen`), and the engine sets the variable to match, so the check box shows what is (a shipped `rs_fullscreen on` reads off at start: a page can't enter full screen without a user gesture).
- At each video restart (the options screen's Apply or Cancel, display-mode.md A.9) a value differing from the page's is asked of it (`full_screen_request`): entered as the offer enters it, the keys locked where the offer would lock them, or left. The restart follows a click or key on the screen, so the browser's transient user activation still holds (documented: HTML's user activation); a gamepad's press gives none, and a refused request leaves the page as it was and the box off. Leaving the game leaves it, as the offer's.
- Where the page has no full screen (`requestFullscreen` missing or `fullscreenEnabled` false: Safari on iPhone, documented by MDN) the check box is disabled and the value kept (options-screens.md 6.3's disabled state).
